# Source and reuse policy

**Purpose:** Career OS is an independent, free toolkit. We do not redistribute or
adapt resources from another creator's paid coaching, course, membership, or
professional program without explicit permission or an applicable license.

## Allowed sources

- Original instructions, prompts, examples, and code independently developed for this project.
- Public LinkedIn posts, public podcasts, and freely accessible commentary by career educators, including creators who also sell courses. **Link to the public original and attribute the source.** Public availability does not grant a license to reproduce substantial content.
- Public hiring research, official reports, and documentation, with attributed and methodologically accurate summaries.
- Independently verifiable professional facts, after removing identifying and confidential details and checking reuse rights.

## Excluded sources

- Paid-program resume workbooks, especially experienced-CSM and healthcare-, education-, or other industry-specific metric-example workbooks.
- Course-specific checklists, metric banks, worksheets, exercises, templates, prompt packs, frameworks, and instructions from any paid professional coaching program.
- Rewordings, derivative templates, or reorganizations based on excluded paid materials.
- Mixed or consolidated career records containing excerpts or adapted material from paid resources **unless the exact source sections have been audited and cleared**.
- Private chats, contact databases, unpublished recruiter notes, customer records, and private job-seeker documents without clear authorization and a legitimate need.

This policy concerns **source provenance and rights**, not whether an individual
creator may be discussed or linked to publicly.

We can still build a new, independently written accomplishment-discovery guide
using widely understood principles, independently verified facts, and original
fictional examples.

## Before incorporating any external content

1. Identify the true original source. A copied file retains its restrictions.
2. If the origin is uncertain, do not import it. A derivative may look different while reproducing paid material.
3. Never copy a workbook's distinctive examples, structure, exercise sequence, or metric bank without a grant of rights.
4. Use public URLs and dates only when attribution is appropriate; do not expose private Drive links or document IDs.
5. Review additions manually. Automated filename tests are useful **only as a limited guardrail** and cannot prove provenance.

## Automation limits

Research and promotion watchers use public source URLs from explicitly configured
allowlists. They do not access private Google Drive folders, coaching materials,
or users' personal job-search data. Any extension to private sources requires
separate approval and source-by-source provenance review.
