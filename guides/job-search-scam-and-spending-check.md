# Job Search Scam & Spending Check

**Someone contacted you about a job. Before you click, pay, or share sensitive information, spend two minutes checking what is actually being offered.**

A legitimate-looking LinkedIn profile, real job posting, or real recruiter is **not by itself proof** that a particular opportunity, payment request, or side service is legitimate.

This guide is free. It does not collect your information. It is written primarily for U.S. job seekers, with official U.S. reporting links below.

## 1. The two-minute recruiter check

- **Confirm the job exists.** Find the exact opening on the employer's official careers site. A copied real posting can still be used in a scam.
- **Verify the person independently.** Find the employer or recruiting agency through its own verified website, then contact them using a phone number or email **you** found, not solely the one in the message.
- **Confirm the relationship.** Ask whether they are recruiting for that employer and whether the employer knows about this particular opening and outreach.
- **Ask what is being sold.** Is the person arranging an interview or offering a separate, optional paid service? Ask explicitly whether payment is a condition of being considered.
- **Protect personal data.** Don't send Social Security numbers, bank details, identity-document scans, passwords, or sensitive personal records in response to an unsolicited pitch. Genuine hiring processes may need some sensitive information at a later, verified onboarding stage.
- **Don't let urgency decide.** Pressure to pay immediately, use a strange payment method, or move off-platform without verification is a reason to stop and check.

### A real recruiter selling a side service is a separate concern

There are at least three distinct situations:

| What happens | How to respond |
| --- | --- |
| **Someone impersonates a recruiter or company.** | Stop sharing information. Verify through the real employer's independently found contact details. |
| **A real recruiter offers optional resume writing, coaching, or interview prep.** | Treat it as a separate sales transaction. Ask about their role, any conflict of interest, prices, refund policy, and whether it affects candidacy. Declining should not be treated as evidence you are unqualified. |
| **Someone says a payment, paid report, training, or dossier is required to get an interview or job.** | **Major red flag. Don't pay to advance in the hiring process.** Independently verify with the employer and report suspicious activity. |

Not every paid career service is a scam, and not every recruiter offering one has bad intentions. **The problem is when the boundaries between recruitment and selling become unclear, especially for people under financial pressure.**

### The recruiter-to-resume-service pivot: an anonymized example

A job seeker described this sequence: a professional approached them as a recruiter, asked for a resume, salary range, start availability and work preferences, and said the resume had entered a screening process. **No specific employer, job opening, or requisition number was identified.** Later, the contact said the resume would probably be rejected and introduced a resume-writing specialist. After the job seeker declined, they offered free resume and LinkedIn optimization.

**What this does and doesn't prove:**

- Gathering qualifications and asking about compensation can be normal recruiting work.
- A generic warning that your resume will be rejected, without naming a role or showing job-specific criteria, is **not a substantiated hiring assessment**.
- Moving from an apparently active recruiting discussion to resume services can be a **sales or lead-generation pivot**, whether the first offer is paid or free.
- A profile headline or job title that changes later is **not proof** the earlier outreach was fraudulent. People change roles.
- This example **does not establish impersonation, a scam, or wrongdoing**. The opportunity and any claimed employer relationship simply remained unverified.

**The useful question to ask before investing more time:**

> Before we discuss resume services, which specific company and role are you considering me for? Is there an active requisition, and are you authorized to recruit or submit candidates for it? I am interested in genuine openings. Please also clarify whether any resume or LinkedIn service is entirely separate from the recruiting process.

If you receive no specific, independently verifiable hiring opportunity, treat the exchange as **unverified networking or a possible services pitch**, not proof that an employer has reviewed or rejected your candidacy.

## 2. Copy this message before you pay or provide documents

> Thanks for reaching out. Before proceeding, could you confirm the employer, exact role or requisition number, and your recruiting relationship with the company? I'd also like to confirm whether the service you've mentioned is entirely optional and whether purchasing it has any bearing on interview consideration. I prefer to verify opportunities through the employer's official channels.

If their answer is evasive, pressuring, or contradicts the employer's response, pause.

## 3. Keep actual spending separate from unemployment's financial impact

A large headline can combine **money paid out** with **income not earned**. Both can be painful; they are not the same financial measure.

Example from a circulating, user-supplied social-media screenshot (not independently audited):

| Category | Reported amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Resume writer | $450 |
| LinkedIn profile optimization | $250 |
| Job-search subscriptions | $475 |
| Mock interviews | $600 |
| **Out-of-pocket service costs** | **$1,775** |
| Estimated lost wages | $50,000 |
| **Combined financial impact** | **$51,775** |

The post's **$51,775 figure is not $51,775 spent on job-search services.** Distinguish actual cash expenditures, avoided costs, opportunity costs, lost earnings, and any reimbursements.

### Your personal spending check

Copy into a note or spreadsheet:

| Date | Product/service | Paid or recurring? | Amount | What it should achieve | Did it help? | Cancel/review date |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| [date] | [name] | [one-time/monthly] | [$] | [concrete result] | [yes/no/unknown] | [date] |

Before buying, ask yourself:

1. What specific outcome am I paying for: expert feedback, time saved, access, or convenience?
2. Is a **free alternative** already sufficient for this task?
3. Can I independently verify the provider's credentials and refund terms?
4. Does the service require access to my personal accounts or private data?
5. If it auto-renews, have I added a calendar reminder to cancel before the charge?

Paying for help can be a rational choice. Buying a promise under pressure is not a strategy.

## 5. If you've already sent money or sensitive information

- Stop further payments and save the listing, messages, email headers, URLs, and payment receipts.
- If you paid, contact the payment provider promptly to report suspected fraud and ask about dispute or recovery options.
- If you shared sensitive identity or financial information, go to [IdentityTheft.gov](https://www.identitytheft.gov/) for situation-specific recovery steps.
- Report suspected job scams to the [FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/). You can also report internet-enabled fraud to [FBI IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/).
- Report the suspicious account or listing to the relevant job board or social platform.
