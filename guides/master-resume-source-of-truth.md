# Build your master resume once (then stop starting over)

**One detailed career record. One strong baseline resume. Small, honest adjustments for relevant jobs.**

A *master career record* is the private source of truth for your experience. It can be longer than a normal resume. Your *baseline resume* is the shorter, readable version you can actually send. When a job is a strong match, you adapt that baseline using facts from the master record instead of inventing a new version of yourself.

**Time:** Start with 15–30 minutes if you already have a resume; add missing evidence over time. **Cost:** $0. **Coding:** none. **Required AI:** none.

**Don't let setup delay your application.** If you need to apply today, use the [one-job quickstart](../START_HERE.md) first and build this record later.

## Why this is useful

- Stop rewriting your work history for each application.
- Identify your real strengths with specific examples, not just adjectives.
- Preserve verified results, numbers, dates, scope, and context.
- Notice adjacent roles that use the same capabilities even when job titles differ.
- Compare actual requirements against your evidence **before** spending an hour applying.
- Reuse reliable stories for resumes, interviews, networking and LinkedIn.

## Step 1: Store your record **privately**

Open a personal document, spreadsheet, or plain text file that *you control*. Copy the [fill-in master career record template](../templates/master-career-record.md) into it.

**Never put your completed master resume into this public GitHub repository.** The repository contains only blank tools and fictional examples.

Remove home address, contact details, identification numbers, salary history, private client/customer data, sensitive health information, confidential employer records, and other information you do not want to share before pasting any part into an external AI assistant. An AI provider has its own privacy rules.

Use your current resume, an old resume, notes, annual reviews you have permission to use, and your own recollections. Don't copy someone else's paid course, workbook, metric bank, or proprietary templates.

## Step 2: Capture facts, not just impressive phrases

For each job, collect:

| Capture | Example of the kind of information to keep |
| --- | --- |
| Role and dates | Official title; employer type; month/year dates; promotions |
| Scope | Team or customer size; regions; tools actually used; budget or responsibility *if known* |
| Work | What you actually owned, delivered, improved or helped with |
| Results | Before → after → time period, when verified |
| Contribution | What **you** did versus what the team or company achieved |
| Evidence status | **VERIFIED**, **RECOLLECTION / CHECK**, or **UNKNOWN** |
| Skills and strengths | A specific example that demonstrates each one |
| Stories | Situation → action → result → lesson |
| Relevant context | Contract work, career changes, transitions, or overlapping jobs explained accurately |

**Do not manufacture percentages.** If you know that a process became faster but don't have measurements, describe the observable change plainly. Don't call a team-wide outcome your individual result.

### Two fictional accomplishment examples

- **Verified number (fictional):** "Shortened onboarding from 8 weeks to 5 weeks across 12 client accounts by establishing clear launch owners and dependency reviews." Only use this type of statement when the before/after, scale, and your contribution are supported.
- **No verified number (fictional):** "Built a shared onboarding checklist and launch handoff process so teams could identify blockers before customer kickoff." A defensible nonnumeric result is better than a fictional 37% improvement.

## Step 3: Copy this prompt to build your master record

You can do this manually using the [blank template](../templates/master-career-record.md). If using a free AI assistant, first redact private information.

### Copy this prompt

```text
Help me create a reusable MASTER CAREER RECORD and a concise BASELINE RESUME from only the career information I provide.

This is not a job application. I want a factual source of truth I can maintain and use across many job postings.

MY CURRENT OR OLD RESUME:
[PASTE REDACTED RESUME OR WORK HISTORY]

ADDITIONAL ROLES, PROJECTS, ACHIEVEMENTS OR NOTES (OPTIONAL):
[PASTE]

TARGET ROLE FAMILIES / TYPES OF WORK I ENJOY (OPTIONAL):
[PASTE]

IMPORTANT WORK CONSTRAINTS (OPTIONAL):
[PAY, REMOTE/HYBRID, GEOGRAPHY, SCHEDULE, TRAVEL]

RULES:
- Preserve official job titles, employers, chronology and credentials.
- Never invent metrics, percentages, titles, team sizes, tools, achievements, degrees or certifications.
- Separate VERIFIED FACTS, MY RECOLLECTIONS TO CHECK, and MISSING INFORMATION.
- Ask targeted questions only for consequential missing details. Do not hold the whole draft hostage to unanswered questions.
- For metrics, record baseline, outcome, time period, scope, my contribution, and verification status when available.
- Distinguish accomplishments I owned from collective team outcomes.
- For each stated strength, connect it to a real situation or accomplishment.
- Infer transferable skills cautiously and label inferences.
- Do not use health, family status, age or other sensitive characteristics in career-fit judgments.
- Do not claim that the resulting resume will bypass an ATS or guarantee interviews.
- Use plain English, not corporate buzzwords.

RETURN:
1. MASTER CAREER RECORD: complete chronological experience, responsibilities, actual scope, projects, tools, education/credentials, factual achievements, skills, and evidence status.
2. ACCOMPLISHMENT BANK: reusable bullet candidates with source evidence and a VERIFIED / CHECK / UNKNOWN marker. Put missing numbers in [VERIFY], never guess them.
3. STRENGTHS WITH PROOF: 4–6 capabilities I can substantiate with examples.
4. POTENTIAL ROLE FAMILIES: 3–5 realistic role families based on demonstrated experience; distinguish proven fit from stretch options.
5. BASELINE RESUME: a readable, conventional 1–2 page starting resume where feasible, with summary, roles, selected achievements and relevant skills.
6. VERIFY NEXT: the 5 highest-value questions or missing facts worth resolving, prioritized for accuracy and usefulness.
7. MAINTENANCE: a short checklist for updating this record after a new project or role.

Keep the master record detailed enough to reuse. Keep the resume concise. Do not discard valid older experience from the master record just because it is omitted from the resume.
```

## Step 4: Evaluate the job *before* rewriting anything

Use your career record with [the fit-and-gaps prompt](../prompts/02-fit-and-gaps.md), or follow the [one-job quickstart](../START_HERE.md).

For each real posting, ask:

1. What are the **actual must-haves**, and which are merely preferred?
2. What documented experience proves I meet those requirements?
3. Is adjacent or transferable experience genuinely plausible, and what's the evidence?
4. What is an actual gap versus something I simply haven't documented?
5. Does the job fit my pay, schedule, location, and other non-negotiables?

Decide **Apply / Investigate / Skip** with clear reasons. A missing preferred qualification is not an automatic rejection; likewise, job-title similarity does not establish fit. No invented ATS scores or percentages.

## Step 5: Tailor lightly, not endlessly

For a strong-fit posting:

- Start from the **baseline resume**, not a blank page.
- Pick the most relevant **verified** accomplishments from your master record.
- Make a small number of changes to the summary, top skills, and a few bullets to reflect the posting's real priorities.
- Use accurate job-language synonyms **only when they describe work you actually did**.
- Preserve original employer names, titles, dates and results.
- Save the submitted copy with the job description or a note about the role, so you can explain it during interviews.

Use the [targeted resume prompt](../prompts/03-resume-positioning.md) if helpful.

**Timebox it:** If you've already shown the top requirements clearly, stop editing and apply or reach out. Resume tailoring shouldn't become a second unpaid job.

## Maintain, don't rebuild

After a meaningful project, promotion, qualification, or quantified result, add the new evidence to your **private master record**. Review it every few months or at the start of a new search. Update your shorter baseline resume only when relevant.

**The goal:** Stop trying to sound qualified for everything. Build a trustworthy record of what you're good at, use it to find roles that genuinely fit, and communicate that fit clearly.
